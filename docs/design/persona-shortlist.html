Reminderski — the shortlist

Three survivors, pushed further. The big face gets eyes that widen and pan across a wider field, so it can look around rather than only glance; it gets a brow pair that moves independently, which turns out to be where nearly all the expression lives. The note gets four ways of being a piece of paper. The bracket gets room to move, arms, and its own brows.

Worth saying up front: the bare big face and the bracket with brows are the same design at two sizes. Same brows, same eyes, same mouth, no body either way. If both keep appealing, what you are actually choosing is how much of the notification the character should own — not which character it is.

Big face

The eye field is now nine characters wide, so the pair can sit centre, drift one column, or move two — which reads as the head turning rather than the eyes flicking. Try Looking around. Three bodies: the drawn head, a rounded one, and no head at all, where the notification's own hairline box becomes the face.

What the brows buy

Twelve still poses, all the same body, differing only in brows, eyes and mouth — three rows of nine characters. Every one of these costs a single line in persona.rs. This is the argument for keeping brows: without them the face has about four states, with them it has as many as we care to name.

Note

Four ways to be a piece of paper: what holds it up, and what the bottom edge does. The Ruled one is the idea worth arguing about — the note carries the face and the reminder written under it, which is the only candidate on either page whose body means something rather than just holding a face.

Bracket

The MVP one. Roomy widens the brackets so the face can slide between them — the same look-around, in one line. Brows adds the second row and, with it, most of the big face's range. Hands gives it something to throw up when ignored.

Where I would land

The bare big face. It is the only one that does not draw a second rectangle inside the rectangle the notification already draws — the face box becomes the head, which is one less line of ink and reads cleaner at a glance. It keeps the brows, the widening eyes and the full pan.

If that feels like too much presence, Bracket / Brows is the same thing shrunk to two lines, and it is the one you already recognise. Nothing is lost except scale: same vocabulary, same moods, a quarter of the ink.

The note is the one I would drop, unless the Ruled version lands for you. A piece of paper with a face on it is two ideas competing for the same 152 px, and the pin and the torn edge never move, so most of the drawing is dead weight.

What changed since round one

  • Eyes gained wide and huge, and a turn two columns off centre on top of the one-column glance.
  • Brows became a pair that moves independently: level, raised, one raised, drawn together, drawn apart, drooping.
  • Every mood now names a brow as well as eyes and mouth, so all eleven are driven by the same script and nothing is hand-tuned to flatter one candidate.

Still true

  • Every frame of a candidate is the same rectangle of characters. Verified, not assumed.
  • Plain ASCII in Consolas. Nothing here sends Windows hunting in a substitute font.
  • The timer runs only while a notification is on screen.
